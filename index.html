A few gateways carry a lot of the traffic.

Australia's population centres are widely separated. Its largest airports connect those cities, while smaller airports extend the network along the coast and into the interior.

SOURCES & METHODS

A note on the numbers.

Government statistics

  • BITRE Domestic Aviation Activity, 2025–26: Tables 1–5 and 7, published pages 3–11 and 23. Airport and route comparisons use financial years ending June 2025 and June 2026.
  • BITRE Australian Infrastructure and Transport Statistics Yearbook 2025: Tables 6.1, 6.2, 6.3a and 6.3b, published pages 138–141. Historical airport comparisons include domestic and international services.
  • ABS National, State and Territory Population, March 2026 release: Table 4, Persons by state and territory. The December 2025 observations are used to match the midpoint of 2025–26.

Geography and preparation

  • OurAirports supplies airport coordinates and state identifiers. Thursday Island is plotted at its serving Horn Island airport. Burnie is plotted at Wynyard airport.
  • Natural Earth supplies simplified state boundaries. Both geographic datasets are public domain. All three maps use an Australia-centred Albers equal-area projection.
  • Source values are retained; figures in thousands are converted to counts. Derived rates, shares and comparisons are documented in Data notes. No missing values are filled with invented figures.

Coverage: 58 distinct airports, formed by deduplicating BITRE's top-ten and top-fifty-regional tables. They represent 98.1% of national domestic airport passenger movements in 2025–26. “Regional” in the source follows ABS geography; this page does not assume that all state capitals are non-regional. All financial-year labels refer to 1 July–30 June. Historical tables and the latest report can differ because of revisions and rounding.
